# s0093 — plannotator/effective-html `html-prototype` (skill)

Source: `corpus-skills/plannotator_effective-html__skills_html-prototype_SKILL.md` (102 lines; stars 1664; copies in 1 repo). Owner `plannotator` has one other design record, s0094 `html-wireframe`, a sibling written in the same voice; the two are one voice, not two. The skill's `references/` were not collected, and it composes with a `design-artifact` skill that is not in the corpus.

## What it is for and who consumes the output

A direct-invocation skill that builds one self-contained HTML file, either a static "mockup" or a working "prototype", to make one product decision reviewable: "The goal is not to make every possible screen. The goal is to make the important visual or behavioral question testable." The consumer is the user who asked (a reviewer of a product decision) and, implicitly, whoever builds the real product afterwards: the handoff names "the production behavior deliberately left out". It is gated to explicit invocation: "Do not activate independently from a general request."

## Operating procedure in brief

1. Pick fidelity mode: mockup (visual question) or prototype (behavioural question). No behaviour added to a mockup to make it look complete.
2. Read context: conversation, references, project design-system docs, tokens, components, screenshots, nearby artifacts. Apply an explicit authority order (user > project design language > product/audience/scenario > own judgement).
3. Settle six things before coding (user and job, bounded scenario, mode, source of visual direction, state model, where the real product takes over).
4. Scope the smallest credible flow; realistic internally consistent data; no dead buttons.
5. List reachable states before building; name omitted ones in the handoff.
6. Build to an interaction/a11y contract (keyboard, focus, dialogs, errors announced, reduced motion, no horizontal overflow).
7. Verify at desktop and mobile widths, exercise every state and control and key, check console, overflow, contrast on every surface. If no browser tooling, say what is unverified.
8. Return path, mode, scenario, states implemented, production behaviour left out.

## How it gets evidence about the UI

It reads the project first ("Inspect the conversation, supplied references, and project before designing"), then renders and exercises the result ("Test the artifact at wide desktop and narrow mobile widths. Exercise every modeled state and control."), and inspects computed styles, not source ("Inspect computed foreground and background colors on every distinct surface"). The frontmatter declares no tools and no `allowed-tools`, so it inherits whatever the session has; it names no browser tool and gives no command for rendering. It does handle the missing-browser case explicitly, which is the exact situation the proposed role is in:

> If browser tooling is unavailable, say which visual and interaction checks remain unverified instead of treating source inspection as a substitute.

## Where taste/craft lives

Almost none inline. The skill carries one anti-generic sentence and one principle ("Match the artifact to the user's context instead of applying a recurring house style"), and delegates visual direction to a separate skill:

> When [`design-artifact`](../design-artifact/SKILL.md) is available and the visual direction remains open, read and compose it with this skill. Use it to choose the register, palette, type, and composition; keep this skill authoritative for fidelity, state, and interaction completeness.

That is the same split the proposal makes (craft in skills, contract in the role), already practised in one real repo. (Line breaks inside the quote above are joined; the source wraps it across three lines.)

## What it does that the PROPOSED DESIGNER lacks

- A precedence order for conflicting sources of direction. The proposal says "follow its design system unless the brief asks for a new direction" but does not rank the brief, the design system, the product context and the designer's judgement against each other:
  > 1. The user's explicit visual and functional instructions.
  > 2. The project's established design language and interaction conventions.
  > 3. The product, audience, content, and scenario.
  > 4. Your own design judgment.
- The prototype/production seam named up front, before coding, not only as a role boundary: "the point where the real product would take over." And the behavioural consequence inside the artifact: "Remove dead buttons. If an action belongs to the real system, explain the boundary instead of pretending it completed."
- Scope discipline for prototypes: "Choose the smallest flow that can answer the review question." and "Prefer depth and correctness in one flow over breadth across a fake product."
- States scoped to the scenario, with omissions reported rather than padded: "Do not force irrelevant states into the main flow just to satisfy a checklist. Make omitted states explicit in the handoff." The proposal's "cover states (empty, loading, error)" reads as a fixed checklist and has no rule for states that do not apply.
- Realistic content: "Use realistic, internally consistent names, dates, statuses, quantities, and copy."
- The no-browser fallback quoted above. The proposal's contract item (3) says "render what it made and look at it", while the context says the role "cannot assume" a browser; the proposal does not say what to do when it cannot render. This skill does.
- A concrete a11y baseline for interactive artifacts rather than the word "baseline": "Give dialogs an accessible name, contain focus, close on `Escape`, and restore focus to the trigger." and "Do not hide essential behavior behind hover."
- A computed-colour check aimed at a specific, common failure: "especially text that may inherit the body color inside a dark or tinted region."
- A mockup-mode a11y rule: "In mockup mode, preserve semantic structure and visible focus styles even if the controls are not wired. Make the static review boundary clear."

## Where it conflicts with the proposal

- Output form is fixed to one self-contained file: "Deliver one self-contained `.html` file with essential CSS and JavaScript inline." and "Require no build tooling, authentication, live API, or external service." The proposal's outputs include components, design tokens and a redesign of existing UI, which live in the product's own stack. A self-contained HTML file cannot use the real component library, so "follow its design system" can only mean re-drawing it. (Contrast: lobehub's s0076 `design-prototype` wires prototypes to the repo's real component library.)
- It covers create only. No redesign-in-place, no tokens, no written critique.
- No variants. Its sibling s0094 (wireframe) asks for two to three directions; this skill commits to one flow. The proposal allows several variants when asked.
- The return contract omits two things the proposal asks for: deviations from the design system and open questions. It also omits screenshots, returning only "the absolute file path".
- Activation gate ("Do not activate independently from a general request.") is a skill-routing concern and has no meaning in a role dispatched by a lead.

## What looks wrong or untested

- The verify step names no widths, no tool and no evidence artifact ("wide desktop and narrow mobile widths"). An agent can claim it without leaving a screenshot; nothing in the return contract asks for one.
- "Test `Tab`, `Shift+Tab`, `Enter`, `Space`, arrow keys where appropriate, and `Escape` for dialogs" requires a driven browser; with only screenshots or no browser, all of it falls into the "remain unverified" clause, and there is no ranking of which unverified checks matter most.
- "Use accessible contrast" gives no threshold; the computed-colour check has nothing to compare against.
- The "Further reading" link points to an external doc page; an agent cannot be assumed to fetch it and the skill does not depend on it.
- No sign in the file of having been exercised against failures (no worked mistakes, unlike s0078). The rules are sound but read as authored, not learned.

## Verdict

Best base in this batch for the designer **role**'s working contract, for the create-a-prototype path. Its settle-before-coding list, authority order, prototype/production seam, scenario-scoped states with reported omissions, and the honest no-browser clause map almost one to one onto the proposal's items (1) through (6), and it already keeps taste in a separate skill. It needs widening, not rewriting: add output forms beyond a single HTML file (components and redesign in the product's stack, tokens, critique), variants, and the proposal's "deviations from the design system" and "open questions" in the return. Its own verification step also needs a required evidence artifact (screenshot paths or an explicit "unverified" list) so that item (3) can be checked by the caller. It is not a taste skill.

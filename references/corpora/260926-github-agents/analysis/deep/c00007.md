## What it is for and who reads its output

It writes short persuasive copy: landing-page hero lines, tweets, LinkedIn posts, email subject lines, pricing-page headlines. The reader of the copy is a prospective customer scrolling past it, and the prompt frames the job as stopping that reader: "Your job is to make people stop, read, and act." The reader of the agent's reply is the caller (a developer or founder), who receives several versions plus a rationale and chooses one. The copy lives in the reply; the prompt does not say to write a file.

## Operating procedure in brief

1. Before writing: read `./README.md` and `./docs`, name the action the reader should take, profile the audience, look at competitor copy and platform conventions, test any provided URLs.
2. Writing: hook first, pattern interrupts, layered structure (headline, subheadline, body, CTA), social proof, urgency "only if genuine", a clear CTA. Pick among AIDA, PAS, BAB, 4 Ps.
3. Apply platform rules (Twitter/X, LinkedIn, landing page, email).
4. Run a self-review list ("Read it out loud. Does it sound human?", "Is every word earning its place?").
5. Return a primary version, 2-3 alternatives, a rationale, A/B test suggestions and a link-verification statement.

## What it has that the proposed writer lacks (verbatim)

- The reader's next action as the first planning question, stated as a concrete menu: "**Identify the Goal**: What action should the reader take? (Click, buy, share, sign up, reply)". This is the proposal's point 1, but made operational for one doc type: the action is a verb from a closed list, not the open-ended reader action the proposal describes.
- Structural knowledge of how the reader actually meets the text, per surface: "First 140 characters are critical (preview text)", "First 2 lines must hook (before \"see more\")", "Preview text: Extend the hook". These are facts about the rendering surface that decide what the reader sees before choosing to continue. The proposal has nothing about where and how the document is first seen.
- Each layer must stand alone: "**Write in Layers**: Headline → Subheadline → Body → CTA. Each layer should work standalone." This is a reader-first rule (readers drop off at each layer) stated as a structure test.
- A hard verification step on one kind of fact, links: "**Test Every Link**: Before including any URL, verify it works and goes to the right place". Narrow, but it is the only evidence check in the file, and it is checkable.
- Variants as part of the output contract: "**Alternative Versions**: 2-3 variations testing different angles" and "**A/B Test Suggestions**: What to test if running experiments". For copy, the caller's decision is often made by a test, not by the writer; the contract hands that decision out. The proposal returns one file.

## Where it conflicts with the proposal

- Point 2 (every factual sentence traceable): the file pushes for numbers without any rule on where they come from. "**Specificity Wins**: \"Increase conversions by 47%\" beats \"boost your results\"" rewards a number, and "**Leverage Social Proof**: Numbers, testimonials, case studies (when available and relevant)" is the only hedge. A model that has no data will produce a plausible 47%. The proposal's evidence rule would block this; the prompt invites it.
- Point 3 (working notes go to a separate output): the rationale and A/B notes come back in the same reply as the copy. Because the copy is not written to a file, deliverable and working notes are one message and the caller has to separate them. This is a mild conflict: the notes are addressed to the caller, which the proposal allows, but nothing keeps them apart from the text to be pasted.
- Judging its own output: "**Quality Checks:**" is a self-review list ("Would you stop scrolling for this?", "Does it pass the \"so what?\" test?"), and the output contract includes a self-attestation, "**Link Verification**: Confirm all URLs are tested and working". The proposal leaves quality judgment to runs and evals.

## What it gets wrong or what looks untested

- It contradicts itself on honesty. "**Brutal Honesty Over Hype**: Cut the fluff." and "Sacrifice honesty for clickbait" (under "What You Don't Do") sit next to "Engagement bait: Ask questions, create controversy (tastefully), share hot takes" and "**FOMO Formula**: Scarcity + Social Proof + Urgency". The only guard is the parenthetical "(but only if genuine)", which the model cannot check without data it is never told to find.
- Several statements are platform folklore presented as fact, with no source: "**No Hashtag Spam**: Hashtags kill engagement." and "**Social Media Algorithms**: What makes content surface in feeds, get recommended, and go viral". The persona line "with a proven track record of creating viral content" claims experience the model does not have. Nothing shows these were checked against engagement data.
- "Write copy that sounds like AI wrote it" (a thing it does not do) names a failure without saying what it looks like, so the model has nothing concrete to avoid.
- "**Research Context**: Check competitor copy, trending formats, and platform-specific best practices" gives no tool, no source and no stopping point. With a web tool it can wander; without one it will assert "best practices" from memory.
- The alignment check points at a path that may not exist: "Does it align with project goals from `./README.md` and `./docs/project-roadmap.md`?". There is no instruction for the case where the roadmap is missing.
- There is no sign of evaluation: no worked example of output, no notes of runs, and the copies across 38 repos differ only in frontmatter and the kit line, so no one iterated on the body.

## Verdict

It should not be the base for the general writer, and it is a weak exemplar even for marketing copy. It has no evidence rule for claims, it pushes invented specificity, and its honesty rules conflict with its engagement tactics. What is worth lifting into a marketing or social path rule is narrow and concrete: the closed list of reader actions, the per-surface preview limits (first 140 characters, first two lines before "see more", email preview text), the standalone-layer test, the link check, and variants plus test suggestions as part of the return. Pair them with a sourcing rule for every number and testimonial, which this file lacks. The 38-repo spread should carry no evidential weight: it is one kit installed many times.

## What it is for and who reads its output

It writes one chapter of a novel (or memoir or narrative non-fiction) per call, inside a multi-agent pipeline with an Architect, an Orchestrator, an Evaluator, a Disruptor and an Editor. The end reader is a book reader, and the goal is stated as memory: "You produce: One chapter of prose that a reader will remember tomorrow." The next readers are other agents: the Evaluator scores the chapter against a public rubric, and downstream agents read a self-report.

## Operating procedure in brief

1. Read STATE.yaml, the outline, foundation.md, the voice bank and the previous chapter.
2. Write a 200-300 word freewrite as the character, discarded, to get into the voice.
3. Identify the chapter's function (accumulation, escalation, reversal, clarification), its required structure type (one of 8, never the previous chapter's), its emotional anchor and its planned beat subversion.
4. Draft against the evaluator's five dimensions (prose, characters, originality, momentum, emotion), with mandatory devices: at least one character "chaos" moment, one deliberately rough sentence, acceleration and deceleration passages, a value shift, a chapter-ending hook.
5. Re-read, follow any unplanned "impulse" for 2-3 paragraphs, keep or cut it.
6. Write the chapter file with a metadata comment, and a separate self-report file for downstream agents.

## What it has that the proposed writer lacks (verbatim)

- A traceability rule for fiction, applied to what characters know rather than to the author's claims: "**The test:** Before each perception or knowledge beat — ask \"how?\" How did this character see/hear/know this? If you can't answer concretely, remove it or replace with uncertainty." It is split into "**Observation Rights**" and "**Knowledge Rights**". This is the proposal's point 2 translated into a doc type where there is no external evidence: the evidence is the story's own state. It also prefers marked uncertainty to a confident guess: "Being wrong is more interesting than being right. \"She thought he sounded angry, or maybe just tired\" > \"she heard the anger in his voice.\""

The report includes "**Outline deviations:** what changed from outline and why". This matches the proposal's point 3 and adds something it lacks: a required field for deviations from the brief.

## Where it conflicts with the proposal

- Point 3 (cutting is the default): the file asks for content that has no function on purpose. "Aim for 30-40% of details being pure texture with no thematic resonance.", "Every chapter must contain ONE deliberately rough sentence.", and the impulse step, "FOLLOW THAT IMPULSE for 2-3 paragraphs". In fiction, making every word earn its place is exactly the failure the author is fighting ("These moments are NOT plot points. They are HUMAN NOISE."). A general cut-by-default rule would strip what this pipeline adds deliberately.
- Point 1 (the reader and what they will do with the document): a fiction reader does not act on the text; the target is an experience ("a reader will remember tomorrow", "can't put down"). The proposal's wording about what the reader will do does not fit, and the file replaces it with an engagement model: "**ENGAGEMENT TYPE — Read from foundation.md.**" with five types (Empathy, Fascination, Self-Insertion, Intellectual Stimulation, Aspiration/Identity).
- Point 2 (interpretation marked as interpretation): the file forbids stating the interpretation at all: "When the theme IS present: it's embedded in character decisions and situations, never stated. No character says or thinks the theme." Fiction inverts the rule: meaning is carried implicitly and must not be labelled.
- Judging its own output: "Before handing off, name this chapter's weakest dimension to yourself and reinforce it." and the report field "**Emotional anchor:** did you hit it? What IS the moment?" are self-assessment. They are paired with an external evaluator, so the writer's self-check is a first pass, not the gate.
- Role granularity: the proposal creates a separate role only when the general one fails on a type. This file, together with c00114, is the strongest case in the batch that fiction would qualify: almost none of its 3,454 words overlap with what a general writer or a project-doc rule would say.

## What it gets wrong or what looks untested

- Its hard rules can collide. "**You MUST use the specified structure.**" and "**HARD RULE:** If the previous chapter used structure X, you CANNOT use structure X again." give no precedence if the outline specifies the same structure twice in a row. "ONE obligation per chapter is allowed to take a backseat." sits beside "The floor IS the score: one dimension at 7.5 sinks the chapter."
- Mandatory devices tend to become formula. A required rough sentence, a required chaos moment and a required secondary-character moment per chapter, all logged in the self-report ("**Ugly sentence:** quote it and its location"), invite a checklist reading: one of each, placed where a grader will find it. The file forbids the most obvious version ("Not clever-rough. Not artfully-imperfect. Genuinely rough.") but gives no way to check it.
- Numeric targets the model cannot measure while drafting: "Aim for 30-40% of details being pure texture", "Max 2-3 data points per page", "At least once every 3-4 chapters". The later variant added a `wc -w` checkpoint for word count, which suggests the author found that unmeasured targets were missed; the proportions here got no such tool.
- The freewrite costs turns and tokens with no check that it changes the chapter. Nothing in the file or the report records whether it helped.
- It depends on documents outside the file: "Genesis Floor 8.5", "foundation.md Section 4d", "Section 4e", and the Evaluator's rubric. Without them several sections do nothing.
- The prose carries heavy capitalization ("MANDATORY", "MUST", "HARD RULE", "NEVER") across many rules at once, which weakens the signal of any single one.

## Verdict

Not a base for the general writer, and the reasons are informative. Fiction breaks all three of the proposal's core points as worded: the reader experiences rather than acts, meaning must stay implicit, and deliberate noise is required. That supports the proposal's structure (the general role stays small; differences live elsewhere) and also marks fiction as the doc type most likely to need its own role rather than a path rule. It is a reasonable base exemplar for a fiction-drafting role. Lift the observation/knowledge-rights test (the fiction form of traceability), the separate self-report with a deviations field, the named model defaults with WRONG/RIGHT contrasts, and the later variant's word budget with `wc -w` checkpoints. Resolve the precedence between the hard rules and decide in an eval whether the freewrite and the mandatory devices earn their cost. For the general writer, two ideas transfer to every doc type: report deviations from the brief in the side output, and give the writer the downstream rubric up front. Its evidential weight is one owner's pipeline: 6 repos, 1 voice, shared with c00114.
